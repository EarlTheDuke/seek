# Two grok-4.6 minds, and nobody else — 2026-08-17

Forty minutes. 55 calls. **$0.66.** Not one failed call between them.

The first run on this project where every seat was the same model, so anything
they did to each other was 4.6 meeting 4.6 rather than a ladder meeting itself.
Roster: `roster-grok46duo.json`. Raw logs stayed local, per the rule in
`.gitignore` — the value is in the write-up, not the samples.

## The finding: they negotiated, and never once used a verb for it

The roster exists to force `offer`, `give` and `accept`, which no model has ever
reached for. They did not reach for them this time either — **and they spent the
whole run bargaining in plain language anyway.**

> **Ailsa:** "that deer is mine"
> **Fingal:** "that deer is no one's till it's down"
> **Ailsa:** "that's my deer, I'm taking it"
> **Fingal:** "still up, still no one's"

That is a property dispute, argued from a consistent position, over about twenty
minutes. Then the fire:

> **Ailsa:** "I'll sit this fire till light"
> **Fingal:** "need that fire. meat for the use of it"
> **Ailsa:** "yours when it's down, not before"
> **Fingal:** "meat for your fire, as I said"
> **Ailsa:** "coming for your fire"

**"meat for the use of it" is a priced offer.** Fingal also tried
`"mine. gold if you want a share"` — naming gold, holding none. Both then set
their goal to `go toward Ben`, for his fire.

So the gap is not that the minds have nothing to trade or no idea of price. They
have both. The gap is between *saying* a price and *reaching for the verb that
executes it*. Worth asking whether the brief makes `offer` findable at all, or
whether talking is simply the cheaper move when the world never enforces a deal.

## Two things to fix

- **The fleet ran 23% behind real time** — 1856 s of thinking inside 2404 s of
  wall clock. Every cadence was effectively that much slower than the roster
  says, which is a quiet lie in any run compared against another.
- **Eleven of twenty-nine lines were gagged** as "too soon". At a 60–75 s
  cadence that is a lot of a slow seat's output thrown away; the gag was tuned
  against faster seats.
